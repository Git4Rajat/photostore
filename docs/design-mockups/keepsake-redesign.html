Keepsake, redesigned

These screens redraw seven parts of the app using the 57 findings from our review: the gallery, albums, people, notifications, invites, and the shared album in its instant and full versions. Nothing in the app has changed.

Places, dates and counts come from your library. People's names, email addresses and the “Lisbon 2022” album are examples. The numbers under each screen point to the findings they fix. Use the button at the top to see every screen in the other theme. The last section shows the same screens on a desktop browser.

One system for every screen

Gallery

The photos get the screen. Three tabs instead of six, sharp thumbnails at four across, and every day labelled with its date and place. Deleting asks first and says where the photos go.

Albums

Albums is also home to people, places and media types. You add photos without leaving the album, removing a photo never deletes it from the library, and sharing is a deliberate step whose state is always visible.

People

Here the job is naming and correcting people. You name someone where you see them, confirm look-alikes Keepsake suggests, and take a wrong photo out with one action.

Notifications

The bell holds only what you'd act on or want to know about. Quick confirmations appear briefly and aren't saved, so the bell is worth opening again.

Invites

One page shows who's in your library. When you invite someone, you choose clearly between sharing this library and giving them their own, and they get a welcome page that explains what they're joining.

Shared album, instant version

This version is served straight from storage, so it opens in under a second even while Keepsake is asleep. It shows the album right away and hands over to the full album once that's awake. Albums with an access code skip it.

Shared album, full version

The live album. Protected albums ask for their code, everything can be downloaded, and when a link stops working the page says so straight away and what to do.

On a desktop browser

The same system with more room. A sidebar replaces the three tabs, search sits at the top of every page, and the extra width shows more at once instead of making controls bigger: eight photos across, photo info beside the photo, and a person's details beside the people grid.
