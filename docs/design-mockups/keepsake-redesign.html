Keepsake, redesigned

These screens redraw eleven parts of the app using the 57 findings from our review: the gallery and its filter, albums, people, notifications, invites, sign in, the upload progress bar, the emails Keepsake sends, and the shared album in its instant and full versions. Nothing in the app has changed.

Places, dates and counts come from your library. People's names, email addresses and the “Lisbon 2022” album are examples. The numbers under each screen point to the findings they fix. Use the button at the top to see every screen in the other theme. The last section shows the same screens on a desktop browser.

One system for every screen

Gallery

The photos get the screen. Three tabs instead of six, sharp thumbnails at four across, and every day labelled with its date and place. Deleting asks first and says where the photos go.

One Filter button

The row of All, Photos, Videos, Liked and Date becomes a single Filter button. Everything you can narrow by sits in one panel, the button shows how many filters are on, and the photos get that row of space back. Search stays where it is.

Albums

Albums is also home to people, places and media types. You add photos without leaving the album, removing a photo never deletes it from the library, and sharing is a deliberate step whose state is always visible.

People

Here the job is naming and correcting people. You name someone where you see them, confirm look-alikes Keepsake suggests, and, in a later version, take a wrong photo out with one action.

Notifications

The bell holds only what you'd act on or want to know about. Quick confirmations appear briefly and aren't saved, so the bell is worth opening again.

Invites

One page shows who's in your library. When you invite someone, you choose clearly between sharing this library and giving them their own, and they get a welcome page that explains what they're joining.

Deleting a library

The most serious thing the app can do, so it takes three steps and says clearly what has and hasn't happened. The password is checked inside the dialog, so you know at once whether it was right. Only then does the email get sent, and the dialog doesn't wait for that.

When the library is shared

If another member is part of the library, they have to agree too. The warning, the inbox message and the sent message all say so, so the owner isn't surprised later.

On a desktop browser

The same steps as a centred dialog over the Library page.

Sign in

The first thing anyone sees. One short form, labels that stay put, and error messages that say what to do next. On a desktop the photos get the left half.

Upload progress

While files are going up, you can keep browsing. A small bar at the bottom, on every page, says how far along it is and how long is left. Tap it for detail. You can pause and pick up later. If something fails, it says which files and why, and offers one button to try again. When it finishes, the bell tells you.

After the upload: getting photos ready

Once the last photo is uploaded, Keepsake still has work to do before search and people are complete. The same bar keeps going through it, so one place answers "is it finished yet?". The three background jobs get plain names, and the bar says which of three steps it is on.

Reading your photos

Spotting places, things and text in each photo so it can be tagged. (was: Vision)

Finding people

Matching the same face across photos, so each person has one page. (was: Clustering)

Getting search ready

Making every photo findable by a few typed words. (was: Indexing)

Cancelling, retrying and skipping

When you stop an upload or deal with the files that failed, the bar doesn't just vanish. It shows what is happening, how far along it is, and what you're left with, so you can tell it worked.

Pausing and resuming

Resuming a paused upload is a continuation, not error recovery. It uses the normal upload bar, so nothing looks like it went wrong. The count includes the photos added before the pause, so it never restarts at zero. The "Trying again" layout is kept for files that actually failed.

One upload, one entry in the bell

An upload has a single entry in the notification list for its whole life. It changes in place as the upload is uploading, paused, resumed, finished or cancelled, keeps its place in the list, and never turns into a second entry. A pause is calm: same blue family and a pause symbol, no red. Red is only for photos that really failed. Counts always include photos added before the pause.

Emails

Keepsake sends four kinds of email. Each one says who it's from and why in the first line, has exactly one button, and says how long the link works and what to do if you weren't expecting it. They're shown here in a mail app. Each also has a plain-text version with the same words and the link on its own line.

Shared album, instant version

This version is served straight from storage, so it opens in under a second even while Keepsake is asleep. It shows the album right away and hands over to the full album once that's awake. Albums with an access code skip it.

Shared album, full version

The live album. Protected albums ask for their code, everything can be downloaded, and when a link stops working the page says so straight away and what to do.

On a desktop browser

The same system with more room. A sidebar replaces the three tabs, search sits at the top of every page, and the extra width shows more at once instead of making controls bigger: eight photos across, photo info beside the photo, and a person's details beside the people grid.
