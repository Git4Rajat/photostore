# Wave reliability: incident findings and engineering actions

Audit date: 2026-11-04. Evidence snapshot: approximately Nov 3 23:13–23:18 UTC. Scope: all 61 persisted waves, both libraries, Vision, clustering and indexing. See the [complete incident ledger](wave-incident-ledger-2026-11-04.md) and [processing/wall-time report](wave-processing-times-2026-11-04.md).

## Assessment

The long runs were not explained by inference throughput alone. Confirmed problems include admission of duplicates as new work, global control-row contention exhausting orchestration retries, interrupted work losing its remaining runnable queue delivery, and predecessor publication failures holding up later clustering. There are also real per-source failures. A wave finishing does not mean all its photos succeeded.

Several correctness fixes are already in the deployed image. They need explicit fault-injection qualification; the remaining work is not solved by increasing retry limits or replica counts. Historical evidence does not establish every individual exception, so this audit separates confirmed incidents, measured inefficiency and diagnostic gaps.

## Incident findings

1. **Vision control contention masqueraded as processing failure.** Wave `852ae63d` spent 5h26m from intake start to completion with 55m34s recorded stage execution. A 3h13m40s Vision gap followed exhausted control-row CAS retries. This was not a model taking five hours. Wave `6d61cb0a` repeatedly hit the same `commit_observations.commit` transaction-index-zero conflicts; successful work between conflicts did not prevent accumulated dispatch exhaustion. Repair required a new durable work request. Earlier attribution of this gap to library-lease contention was incorrect. Actions V1/R1.
2. **Wave size overstated new uploads.** Five Nov 2 waves contained 41,389 memberships but only 32,025 distinct photo IDs. The last three contained 21,202 memberships but 11,850 distinct IDs. These counts still do not measure first-ever uploads. Duplicate finalization could enter admission and reset statuses. Some duplicate members reused receipts with zero model work: 1,498 examined in `88397a60`, 623 in `5c061254`. Another examined set of 901 duplicate-finalized filenames in `5c061254` did execute processing steps, including 112.4s aggregate face detection. This does not prove every duplicate reran every model. Action V2.
3. **Rollouts and queue delivery counters stranded clustering.** Three deployments interrupted `88397a60`; checkpoint progress survived, but busy-lease deliveries consumed raw dequeue attempts. Delivery six dead-lettered with only three actual dispatcher executions. Later both leases were expired and no runnable delivery remained. Exact-payload replay restored progress without resetting source state. Timing strongly supports termination at the grace limit; there was no evidence establishing OOM. R1/C1.
4. **Index exhaustion propagated to successors.** `88397a60` exhausted six index dispatch attempts at Nov 3 17:35:39 UTC after a vector-publication checkpoint at about 17:27. Its missing completion publication blocked successor clustering. Retained logs establish a busy-lease error, a later queue error and exhaustion, but do not identify the root of every attempt. It eventually completed at 20:54:06 UTC. I1/I2/O2.
5. **Maintenance competed with ingestion.** A previous ten-hour window had 58,049 tag-maintenance failure records, including 58,040 duplicate `WorkLeased` records. Logical-intent coalescing, active-wave barriers and backoff were necessary; raw log volume was not 58,049 distinct photo failures. R1/V1.
6. **Two replicas did not ensure one wave on each.** One Vision replica could own both libraries while another stayed idle: multiple wave coordinators share a replica's photo-worker pool. Queue length one controls scaling pressure, not coordinator fairness. V4.
7. **Clustering continuation is correct in principle but expensive.** Recent `5c061254` slices applied 128 new assignments while replaying cumulative prefixes of 4,608–4,992 faces. Four slices took 172–180s each; snapshot preparation plus index rebuilding alone cost approximately 73s per slice. Assignment persistence used about 5,500–6,000 storage calls per 128 new assignments. Replaying a growing prefix on every slice risks quadratic total work. These phase timings can overlap and must not be blindly summed. C1/C2.
8. **Terminal member records do not preserve enough diagnosis.** There are 202 records: 124 required-step retry exhaustion, 64 source retry exhaustion, 10 unqualified identities and four operator-release records for replaced sources. Two belong to a cancelled wave; current wave failed-member totals are 200. Today only 68 records match the current source generation; 49 refer to a different generation and 85 have no current source. That does not prove deletion caused their historical failure. Immutable settlements retain a witness digest but discard detailed failed-step evidence. D1.
9. **OCR failures lose their inner error.** Nineteen matching-generation records currently show failed OCR with the generic `ocr_processing_failed` code and no retained underlying message/classification. Thirty-second failed step durations justify investigating timeout behavior, but do not prove all nineteen timed out. Native calls can outlive the retired OCR thread after timeout; whether this materially affected these waves remains unproven. V3.
10. **Abrupt uploads recovered, with ordered waits.** `b906…` and `6a122078…` expired abandoned sessions, cancelled unresolved reservations and ultimately completed with zero failed members. `6a122078` cancelled two unresolved reservations and waited for its predecessor. This is an expected recovery path; the visibility and latency of dependency waits need improvement, not removal of ordering. R1/I2.
11. **My earlier scale change introduced a separate configuration defect.** The CLI update for queue length one produced incomplete scaler metadata. Restoring the CLI-projected configuration omitted scaler identity; revision 71 then reported “no connection setting given.” A raw ARM correction restored system identity in revision 72. This was an operational change error, separate from the pre-existing application failures. O1 prevents recurrence.

## Current deployment versus remaining work

Live source image verified during this audit: `ef23c0e920c3890713e68b66c9d8c103c278ccf0`; Vision revision `0000074`, cluster `0000071`, indexer `0000107`. The fixes through `4268b01` and duplicate admission `2184639` are ancestors of that image. A correct image reference proves deployment identity, not behavioral qualification.

Existing tests in `backend/tests/test_wave_continuation_safety.py` cover live-owner retries, claim races, productive versus no-progress accounting, predecessor waits, handoff failure, index continuation and receipt verification, and deferred/dead-letter copy failures. These are useful existing protections; do not reopen them as missing implementation. This audit did not rerun or fault-inject the deployed apps, so it does not claim production qualification from test names alone.

At the snapshot, hack4heart waves `5c061254`, `01937bc4` and `04b165a3` remained broadly `ready`: Vision had finished, the first was progressing through clustering and later waves waited for predecessor publication. Their member failure counts were 22, 25 and two respectively. A recent error-free log interval does not mean those failed photos recovered.

## Execution order

1. Qualify R1/V1/I1 against the currently deployed code with controlled interruption and send/ACK failures. Establish a release gate covering all three apps, including rolling deployments and scale-to-zero recovery.
2. Implement D1/O2 first so new failures retain their cause. Build the per-member disposition manifest; investigate same-generation failures with no receipt/status resets. Historical missing or replaced sources require source-identity/deletion evidence before repair.
3. Fix C1/C2 and V4 as measured performance/scheduling changes. Each gets before/after timings and correctness/fairness acceptance evidence, not replica-count assumptions.
4. Close V3 error taxonomy and resource control; qualify V2 duplicate/no-work behavior; implement I2 operator visibility and O1 deployment/scaler checks.

Keep the present Container Apps architecture, generation fencing, journals and publication barriers. No periodic worker is required to compensate for dropped handoffs: queue ownership and durable continuation must be sufficient. Do not rerun whole waves indiscriminately, resurrect cancelled/deleted content, or clear failed markers merely to make the dashboard green.

No application code, queue messages, source records or cloud configuration were changed by this audit. It produces an actionable backlog and a complete wave ledger; implementation and live fault qualification remain explicit work.
